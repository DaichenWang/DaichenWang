So the first question is what kind of thing that desire actually is. Fei Xiaotong described traditional Chinese society as Xiangtu, soil-bound: identity, kinship and obligation inseparable from the land you were born on, organised in concentric circles he called Chaxu Geju. Chunyun is its most visible modern expression. But Fei's frame reaches a limit with permanent migrants, whose geographical centre has already moved and is not moving back. So the research shifts from roots to routes, and turns to Avtar Brah. Homing desire, she argues, is not the desire to return to a particular place. Home is a mythic place of desire in the diasporic imagination: a feeling you construct, not a location you reach. And Paul Connerton tells us where that construction happens. Cultural memory is not only in texts and monuments; it is inscribed in the body through habit. Familiar spaces, familiar sounds, familiar ways of moving are the mechanism.
