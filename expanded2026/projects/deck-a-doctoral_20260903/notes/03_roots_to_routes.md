So what kind of feeling is that? Fei Xiaotong described old Chinese society as tied to the soil. He called it Xiangtu. Who you are, who your family is, what you owe people — all of it grew out of the land you were born on, in circles spreading out from you. But that model runs out with people who move away for good, because their centre has already moved, and it is not moving back. So we shift from roots to routes, and turn to Avtar Brah. She calls it a homing desire. It is not the wish to go back to one place. Home, she says, is a place you build in your head, not a place you arrive at. And Paul Connerton tells us where you build it. Culture is not only kept in books and monuments. It is kept in the body, in habit. Familiar spaces, familiar sounds, familiar ways of moving — that is the machinery.
